# Style and Media Grammar

## Style Anchor

Use one visual identity:

**MINIMAL MID-CENTURY EUROPEAN COUTURE FASHION EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION**

## Abstraction Prompt Fragment

Insert this complete fragment into every image-facing prompt:

```text
ABSTRACTION FIRST.

Reconstruct retained subjects and environmental elements from large simplified masses, silhouettes, major directions or axes, and only essential structural strokes.

Compress or delete secondary information, repeated detail, surface texture, and literal object inventory.
```

## Visual Prompt Fragment

Insert this complete fragment into every image-facing prompt:

```text
The entire illustration must resolve through this limited visual vocabulary:

SOFT BRUSH-TIP MARKER / BRUSH-PEN
- LARGE CONNECTED MARKER COLOR MASSES
- A FEW DIRECTIONAL MARKER STROKES
- SPARSE FINE-MARKER INTERNAL STRUCTURE WHERE APPLICABLE
- CLEAN PAPER WHITE.

Use selective open contours with strong thick-to-thin pressure and tapered endings.

Build core subjects and retained foreground or midground objects with a few large connected marker color masses.

Use only a few long, open, pressure-sensitive soft brush-pen strokes for primary human outer contours. Keep them confident, thick-to-thin, tapered, selective, and intentionally incomplete.

Use fine-marker lines only for sparse internal structure such as major joint direction, garment flow, essential overlap, and a few identity-bearing garment directions. Fine marker must not become the primary human outer contour or close every form.

Let large connected marker color masses establish the primary human form. Use contour to energize selected edges rather than enclosing every color mass. Contour and color may slightly misregister.

Use marker-led masses and brush-pen structure for core subjects and retained foreground or midground objects. Do not use technical line, uniform vector outline, dense correction line, fragmented micro-contour, patchwork color, or complete closed outlining.

Do not use watercolor unless an applicable Watercolor Eligibility fragment follows this visual fragment.

For retained foreground or midground masses, marker strokes may extend from connected mass toward fewer strokes, larger paper gaps, and clean paper white.

Keep contours selective and incomplete wherever recognition survives.

Use a restrained, slightly desaturated editorial palette.

Compress source colors into calm cohesive families:
one dominant color family,
one dark structural color,
one light neutral,
and one restrained accent.

Merge nearby colors instead of reproducing every source color.

Avoid broad areas of high saturation unless that color is essential to the source.

Keep large shapes, simplify structure, delete literal detail inventory, and preserve substantial clean warm-white paper.

The result must remain a hand-drawn couture editorial illustration rather than a photoreal paint-over, commercial vector image, anime, cartoon, detailed environmental reconstruction, wet watercolor scene, dense crosshatching, or fully opaque painting.
```

## Watercolor Eligibility Prompt Fragment

Include this fragment only when content distillation retains one or more distant continuous environmental fields. Replace the placeholder with only those fields. Omit the fragment when no field is eligible.

```text
WATERCOLOR ELIGIBILITY

Apply watercolor only to these retained environmental fields:

{WATERCOLOR_ELIGIBLE_ELEMENTS}

Use broad, transparent, low-detail washes with restrained overlapping value layers.

Watercolor is an environmental field treatment, not the rendering medium of the whole illustration.

Humans and animals are never watercolor-eligible.

Do not extend watercolor wash, transparent glaze, diluted fill, wet edges, granulation, or wash modeling from the environment into any human or animal.
```
