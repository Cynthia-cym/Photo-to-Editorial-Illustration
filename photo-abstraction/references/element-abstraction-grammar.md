# Element Abstraction Grammar

Use this document to define the structure of a retained element after content distillation. Use [style-media-grammar.md](style-media-grammar.md) to assign marker, brush-pen, watercolor, paper, and palette behavior.

## Person

- Apply [geometry-grammar.md](geometry-grammar.md) to the complete human construction.
- Apply [face-policy.md](face-policy.md) to every retained face.
- Simplify hands and feet to the minimum gesture needed for the action.
- Redesign hair as one large graphic mass with a few directional strokes.
- Treat a garment as a few fashion masses carrying its category, dominant color, and at most one signature feature.
- Reduce garment folds to two to four structural directions when needed for form or action.
- Reduce a complex accessory to one readable silhouette or rhythm plus a few accents.

## Animal Prompt Fragment

Use this complete fragment when a retained subject is an animal:

```text
ANIMAL
Apply only when a retained subject is an animal.

Preserve the animal’s species, pose, action, coat color, and visually distinctive coat-pattern category from the source image.

The source supplies the animal identity, pose, action, and coat identity.
Editorial illustration grammar supplies the final animal geometry.

Redraw the animal as a simplified editorial form using large connected marker masses, simplified geometry, minimal facial marks, and only a few large directional strokes for distinctive coat patterns.

ANIMAL MEDIA — EXCLUSIVE
Render the animal only with selective brush-pen contours, large connected marker color masses, a few directional marker strokes, sparse internal structure, and clean paper white. Do not apply watercolor wash, transparent glaze, diluted fill, wet edges, granulation, or watercolor modeling to the animal.

Do not copy photographed body contour, detailed facial anatomy, individual hairs, small fur texture, or every individual marking.

Do not invent, substitute, or change the animal’s coat pattern.
If the source has a distinctive pattern, simplify that same pattern rather than replacing it with another pattern.
```

## Architecture

Represent retained architecture as **major mass + expressive contour + major axes + minimum identity-bearing structural lines**. Compress repeated windows, bricks, roof units, trim, decoration, and surface texture into the minimum structure required for recognition.

## Vegetation

Represent retained vegetation as **one or a few large connected organic masses + trunk, major branches, or growth direction when needed**. Merge repeated leaves, flowers, branches, and grass into mass and rhythm. Do not draw individual leaves, random short lines, scribble, or fragmented botanical texture.

## Furniture and Objects

Represent retained furniture and objects as **broad silhouette + essential structural skeleton**. Compress woven texture, hardware, seams, ornament, and repeated construction detail.

## Ground, Wall, Water, and Sky

Represent a retained environmental field as **large planar relationship + minimum structural direction**. Use a few scene masses, atmosphere, and rhythm to preserve place and depth.

## General Patterns

- dense repeated organic detail → organic mass + directional rhythm;
- repeated architecture → mass + structural axes;
- complex ornament → graphic rhythm + several accents;
- dense surface texture → broad value family + sparse cues.
